- Collage Maker is built. Choose up to twelve pictures, put them in the order you want, and get one image back — arranged, drawn and encoded by your own browser, with nothing uploaded. Four arrangements: a grid as square as the count allows, a single row, a single column, and a feature that gives the first picture two thirds of the width and stacks the rest beside it. The gap between pictures, the colour behind them, the finished width and the output format are all yours; the height follows from the arrangement, so a taller collage makes a taller file rather than a squashed one. Cells are filled rather than fitted, which is why no cell arrives with two bars of background beside it.
- The preview is not a mock-up of the collage: it is the collage, drawn at full size and shown smaller, so what gets downloaded is the drawing that was already on screen. The pixel limits the other image Gizlets keep are checked against the whole composition rather than against each picture going into it, because twelve pictures that are individually fine still add up, and the canvas that fails is the combined one.
- Collage Maker is a Flow block, and the first one that turns several images into a single image. A flow now takes its ceiling from the combining block in the chain rather than from the category holding it — assembling pages and arranging a collage are different jobs with different numbers of pictures — and a block that combines says what it actually produces, so a collage block reads "one image" where the PDF block reads "one PDF". A recipe link carries the arrangement; the gap, the colour and the width are taste, and a link does not carry taste nobody chose.
