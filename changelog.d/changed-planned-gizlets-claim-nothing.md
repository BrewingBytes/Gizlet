- Nothing on the site now describes an unbuilt Gizlet as working, and the compiler keeps that rather than a convention keeping it. A registry entry is either available, with an implementation behind it and an agent-facing description of what it reads and writes, or planned, with neither and no locality claim at all — locality is a claim about an implementation, and an unbuilt Gizlet has none. An unbuilt Gizlet's page renders no advertisement in any placement, stays out of search results and out of the sitemap, publishes only the breadcrumb trail rather than markup announcing a free working application at that address, recommends nothing, and appears in no search suggestion, no home page row and no other Gizlet's related list.
- The machine-readable catalogue now describes the plan as well as the product, which reverses an earlier rule deliberately: a catalogue that lists only what exists cannot answer "is this coming?", and the honest answer to that is more useful than silence. `/tools.json` publishes unbuilt Gizlets under their own `planned` key and `/llms.txt` under its own heading, both saying outright that nothing there is available. The shape omits the locality field, the input and output descriptions, and the privacy sentence rather than publishing an empty or false value for each: nothing claims locality, nothing denies it, and a reader is never asked to work out that a key name means "do not call this", because the fields that would let it be called are simply absent.
- A kill-criterion label reads on a dark background as well as a light one. `--warning` joins `--success` as a themed token with a lighter dark-mode value, so the one line on the site that says what would make us stop is not the hardest line to read.
