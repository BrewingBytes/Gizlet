- Image Color Picker is built. Pick a pixel out of a picture and read it as HEX, RGB and HSL, each copyable — drawn onto a canvas and read one pixel at a time by your own browser, with nothing uploaded and no file written. Click or tap a pixel, drag to keep picking as you sweep across an area, or focus the picture and move with the arrow keys: one pixel a step, ten with Shift, stopping at the edge. It opens on the middle pixel, so there is always a colour to move from rather than an empty panel to aim at.
- The colours picked during a visit sit underneath, newest first, each copyable by clicking it. Picking the same colour twice moves it rather than filling the row with one mistake. They live in the page and nowhere else: closing the tab forgets them, and nothing is written to browser storage or sent anywhere.
- A grey is reported with a hue of zero, because every hue produces a grey once the saturation is gone and naming one would be inventing it. The swatch decides whether to print its own hex in dark or light text by relative luminance rather than by lightness, which is the difference between a readable label on yellow and a white one.
